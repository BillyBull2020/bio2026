## The problem

### You don't have an agent problem. You have a decision problem.

Most AI sales stacks are a room full of personalities with different scripts and no manager.
Add a "closer" agent. Add a "review" agent. Add a "follow-up" agent. Fourteen agents later,
nobody can say why a specific message went to a specific person on a specific day.

The cost is not the API bill. It is:

- **Misfired pressure.** The wrong message at the wrong moment, at scale.
- **Duplicate logic.** Every persona re-implements memory, tone, consent and safety.
- **No audit trail.** When a customer complains, there is no defensible answer.

Choice OS replaces the roster with a kernel. The persona becomes a voice. The decision
becomes the product.

## How it works

### 1. Read the decision, not the persona

Six resources, each estimated from evidence — calls, replies, objections, bookings, reviews,
silence — and decayed toward a prior so a month-old lead is not treated as hot.

### 2. Find the binding constraint

The constraint is the resource that is both most lacking and most movable right now. A
frightened customer is not "one micro-commitment away" from yes. A price objection is not a
reason to send a closer; it is a clarity problem with a specific answer.

### 3. Choose the smallest legal move

Sixteen moves, each with a target, a pressure cost and a guard. The scheduler scores
expected readiness gain against pressure spent. If no move clears the floor, it waits — and
logs the wait like any other decision.

### 4. Pass the rails before anything is sent

Consent, quiet hours, frequency, pressure budget, claim trace, real urgency, review
integrity, AI disclosure, vulnerable-contact escalation. The rails run before generation and
again after. A language model cannot talk the kernel out of a block.

### 5. Log everything

Every decision, every alternative, every block and every opt-out goes into a SHA-256-chained
event log. Replay it and you get the same book. Show it to a client and you have an audit
trail, not a promise.

## The rails are the product

Anyone can generate a persuasive sentence. Very few can prove that the sentence was allowed.

- **Claim trace.** Every dollar, percentage and large number resolves to a source with an
  expiry. No source, no sentence.
- **Pressure budget.** Every contact has 3.0 units. High-pressure moves spend them. The
  budget is a ceiling, not a target.
- **No threat without a door.** A loss statement is legal only when it is evidenced and
  paired with a concrete next step.
- **Review integrity.** Every completed job is asked. No gating, no star instructions.
- **Human override.** Vulnerable contacts and high-pressure moves go to a person.

This is what makes the system sellable to a business owner who has to look a customer in the
eye.

## FAQ

**Is this just a CRM?**
No. A CRM stores what happened. Choice OS decides what should happen next, under constraints,
and records why.

**Does it use an LLM?**
The kernel does not need one. Copy is deterministic and claim-bound. A model can rewrite
tone, but it cannot add a number, a deadline or a guarantee; the policy gate runs again on its
output.

**What happens when the customer says stop?**
Global, immediate suppression. Every non-transactional channel is blocked, and the attempt is
logged.

**How do you know the neuroscience is real?**
We do not claim to measure neurotransmitters. We model six enabling conditions — safety,
trust, anticipation, clarity, agency, commitment — from observable events, and we publish the
constructs, the citations and the caveats. The model is falsifiable; the pilot is the test.

**Can I keep my current tools?**
Yes. Twilio, Stripe, Supabase, your calendar and your voice pipeline become adapters. The
kernel decides; the tools deliver.

**What does it cost?**
The OS is the engagement. Pricing is per book of business, not per seat. Start with the
Reviver pilot on your existing customer list.
