# Competitive Position

## The category mistake

The market sells **more leads**: Angi, Thumbtack, Google Ads, lead brokers, purchased lists.
The contractor's real problem is that the leads already paid for are not worked.

Choice OS competes on **revenue recovered from the existing book**, not on lead volume. That
changes the buyer, the price, the proof and the ethics.

## Alternatives

| Alternative | What it does | Where it fails | Our wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead marketplaces** (Angi, Thumbtack) | Sells shared leads | Price war, low close rate, no customer relationship | Work the customers already owned; no lead cost |
| **Answering services** | Answers the phone, takes a message | No follow-up, no booking, no reactivation | Answers *and* decides the next move, then books |
| **GoHighLevel / CRM automation** | Workflows, pipelines, blasts | Static if/then; no model of why a person buys; easy to spam | Binding-constraint sequencing, pressure budget, policy rails |
| **AI SDR tools** | Personalised cold outreach at volume | Purchased lists, generic claims, deliverability risk, no consent | Consent-first, claim-bound, past-customer reactivation |
| **In-house CSR** | Human follow-up | Inconsistent, expensive, forgets the long tail | Works every contact every day; human handles the warm handoff |
| **“AI agent” platforms** | A general agent with tools | Unpredictable, no audit trail, no guardrails, 14-agent sprawl | One kernel, one event log, one answer to “why did this send?” |

## Why the kernel wins

1. **A causal model, not a script.** Six resources (safety, trust, anticipation, clarity,
   agency, commitment) and a binding constraint. The message changes because the constraint
   changes.
2. **Restraint is measurable.** The pressure budget and the “wait” decision are first-class.
   Fewer sends, fewer opt-outs, more bookings per send.
3. **Every claim is traceable.** No invented review, deadline, price or credential. A claim
   without evidence is refused before it reaches a customer.
4. **Consent is a data structure, not a checkbox.** Channel, purpose, source, timestamp. No
   consent, no marketing — even if the list says “yes.”
5. **One answer to “why?”** The event log is hash-chained and replayable. When a customer,
   regulator or owner asks, the answer is a decision record, not a model's mood.
6. **Humans stay in the loop where it matters.** Price objections, vulnerable contacts, legal
   threats and low-confidence situations escalate.
7. **Hermes is an analyst, not a second brain.** It writes the weekly report and the risk
   list. It cannot send or mutate. No agent sprawl.

## The moat

- **Vertical decision data.** Which constraints actually block a roof, an HVAC replacement, a
  restoration job — and which moves resolve them without discounting.
- **The event log.** Every decision, wait and outcome for every contact. This becomes the
  training set for the next vertical, not a generic prompt library.
- **The claims registry.** A verified library of licenses, proofs and case studies per
  business. Hard to copy, invaluable to the owner.
- **The policy layer.** Consent, quiet hours, frequency, claim trace, review integrity,
  vulnerable-contact handling. This is the part competitors treat as an afterthought.
- **The proof discipline.** Holdout measurement and opt-out guardrails. A competitor can claim
  lift; we can show it.

## Objections and truth

- **“This is just AI spam.”** Then the opt-out rate will show it. The pilot fails on opt-outs,
  not just bookings. The pressure budget is the product.
- **“My customers will be annoyed.”** They are already annoyed by silence and inconsistent
  follow-up. The OS contacts only consented customers, with a specific reason, and stops.
- **“I can hire a CSR cheaper.”** A CSR works 40 hours and forgets the long tail. The OS works
  every contact every day and hands the warm ones to the CSR.
- **“AI cannot sell a roof.”** Correct. It does not try. It opens the conversation, answers the
  objection it can trace, and hands the complex job to a human.
- **“What if the model hallucinates?”** The customer-facing copy is deterministic and
  claim-bound. Hermes is read-only. A model can rewrite tone, never facts.

## The 90-day plan

1. **Days 0–30:** 5 pilots in roofing/HVAC. One vertical, one offer, one holdout.
2. **Days 31–60:** Convert 3 to monthly. Publish two case studies with booked-job numbers.
3. **Days 61–90:** Add calendar and CRM adapters, launch the referral motion, raise price to

The goal is not to be the cheapest. It is to be the only system that can prove it recovered
revenue without burning the list.
