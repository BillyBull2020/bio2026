# Sales Emails and Call Scripts

The buyer is an owner-operator, not a marketing manager. Write like a person who looked at
their business, not like an agency.

## Build the list (before writing)

- Roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, restoration.
- 5–50 employees, owner still quotes.
- Denver metro first, then one adjacent market.
- Source: Google Maps, state license lookup, company website, LinkedIn.
- Find the owner's name and direct email. No purchased email lists.
- Do not cold-text. Email and call only; SMS requires consent.

## Warm email (referral or local connection)

**Subject:** the calls you missed last month

> Hi Ray —
>
> I’m local, and I work with roofing companies on one specific problem: the calls and unsold
> estimates that never get followed up.
>
> Not leads. The customers you already paid to acquire.
>
> I built a small system that reads the book, decides who is actually ready for a next step,
> and writes the message — with a hard stop on anyone who opted out. On a 16-customer demo
> book it recovers about $15k in booked work without spraying messages.
>
> Worth 15 minutes to see if your missed-call number is big enough to matter? I’ll show you the
> math on your own numbers, not a deck.
>
> — Billy
> BioDynamX · Denver
> [phone] · [site]

## Cold email 1 (no referral)

**Subject:** missed calls at [Company]

> Hi [Name] —
>
> Quick question: roughly how many calls does [Company] miss in a week?
>
> For most roofers I talk to it’s 10–30. At a $12k average ticket, even a small recovery rate is
> real money — and it’s already in your phone log.
>
> I’m not selling leads. I’m building a system that works the calls and past customers you
> already have, with consent and an opt-out on every message.
>
> If I send you a one-page breakdown of what your missed-call number is worth, will you read it?
>
> — Billy
> BioDynamX · Denver

## Cold email 2 (three days later)

**Subject:** re: missed calls at [Company]

> [Name] — one number and I’ll stop:

> follow-up problem.
>
> I’ll build the one-page math for your business. No pitch, no calendar link maze.
>
> Want it?

## Cold email 3 (one week later)

**Subject:** last note

> [Name] — I’ll leave you alone after this.
>
> If the missed-call number is not a priority, no problem. If it is, reply “math” and I’ll send
> the one-pager.
>
> — Billy

## The 60-second call

> “Hey [Name], this is Billy with BioDynamX here in Denver. I’m not selling leads — I work on
> the calls and past customers you already have. Quick question: how many calls do you think
> you miss in a week?”
>
> *(Listen. Do not talk over the number.)*
>
> “That’s about what I hear. At your average ticket, that’s a real number. I built a system
> that works those calls, follows up with past customers who opted in, and stops the moment
> someone says stop. Would it be crazy to spend 15 minutes looking at your own numbers?”
>
> *(If yes: book a time. If no: “What would need to be true for this to be worth 15 minutes?”)*

## Voicemail

> calls you’re missing and the past customers nobody follows up with. I’ll send a one-page
> breakdown. If it’s not useful, ignore it. Thanks.”

## Referral ask (day 30, if it worked)

> “[Name], the pilot produced [X] booked jobs and [$Y]. Who else in your network has the same
> missed-call problem? I’ll give them the same one-page math, and I’ll take care of you on the
> next month.”

## Don'ts

- No cold SMS. It is illegal without consent and it poisons the number.
- No fake “I noticed your website…” personalization. If you did not look, do not say you did.
- No attachments on the first email.
- No more than three follow-ups.
- No “guaranteed #1 ranking,” “guaranteed revenue,” or invented case study.
- No mass BCC. Send individually or use a proper B2B sequencer with an opt-out.
- If they say stop, stop. The same rule the product enforces applies to the salesperson.
